The problem

Raw $/MTok misleads.

Token-price comparisons assume every model spends the same tokens to do the same job. In practice, the most concise configs finish a complex task with 5–10K output tokens, while verbose configs burn 120–160K — a 15–20× swing that raw pricing never sees.

A verbose model doesn't just cost more per task — it drains the same monthly token pool faster, shrinking your plan's real task capacity.
The method

The task-adjusted formulation

Three numbers turn a raw token quota into purchasing power you can actually compare.

Monthly task capacity
Ntasks= TmonthlyCtask

How many real tasks your monthly token pool can actually solve.

Effective cost per task
Costtask= PmonthlyNtasks

What you actually pay per completed task — fee divided by real capacity.

Tasks per dollar
Tasks$= 1Costtask

The true efficiency metric: completed tasks delivered per dollar spent.

Where T = monthly usable tokens, C = output tokens consumed per task (CursorBench 4.0), P = monthly plan fee. Baseline ranking uses the Medium reasoning tier (Standard for Composer 2.5) — switch tiers live in the ranking below.

Key takeaways

What the shift reveals

The verbosity penalty

Big allowance ≠ real value

gemini-3.8-flash offers tens of billions of tokens and tops raw $/MTok charts — but at 128,364 output tokens per task, its effective task cost falls behind far leaner models.

The coding workhorses

Concise models, huge pools

ChatGPT's gpt-5.6-luna & gpt-5.6-terra pair big monthly pools with ~7,300–7,600 tokens/task for industry-leading yields, while composer-2.5 balances at 17,347 tokens — 5,000+ tasks per dollar on Cursor Ultra.

Quality at moderate overhead

The premium sweet spot

Sonnet 5 (39,114 tok/task) and Opus 5 (45,272 tok/task) trade some efficiency for benchmark quality — climbing past more verbose competitors on effective cost per task.

Coverage gaps

Models awaiting benchmark

40 models 157 plans pending

These models from the real-api-pricing dataset have no CursorBench 4.0 evaluation — their output tokens per task are unknown, so they're flagged [Pending CursorBench] and excluded from the ranking.

API-only rows are excluded by design

API-only plans for benchmarked models (e.g. "Claude Opus 5 API") carry no monthly token quota, so they can't be ranked as subscriptions — their absence is intentional, not a coverage gap.

How a pending model joins the ranking

When CursorBench or a community saturation run publishes completion-token figures for DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM, Qwen, StepFun or MiniMax: update data/model-token-consumption.json and re-run compute_task_ranking.py — the ranking integrates them seamlessly.
